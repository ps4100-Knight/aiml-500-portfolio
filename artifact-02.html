2.4 Adaptability and Change Leadership

614 WORDS
Pruthviraj Surendrasinh SolankiPosted October 30, 2026 at 7:57 PMGroup 4

A leader needs to be highly adaptable to change as its their decisions that influence and shape the productivity and the product that a team creates. Things go wrong all the time, things do not work as expected all the time; if the leadership can not adapt to factors such as new information, changes in market, pivoting of ideas, new tools, technological advancement among other factors the team may be left behind or if the adaptation of change is not carried out properly, confused and counter productive.

One such situation happened during my Master's course, one of the teams in our class tried to pivot from Unity 3D to Unreal engine 5 2 months into a 6 month long game project. The team's production lead was quite talented with unreal engine however the rest of the team was divided due to reasons like not wanting to leave the comforts of Unity 3D based on their existing skills. The decision to pivot game engines meant redoing a lot of work but at the same time it would have made future work much more easier and quality of the final project much better than Unity 3D. However, as the team failed to adapt to Unreal engine 5, which was new at the time the project got off track in terms of production time. By the end it turned into more of a tech demo than a video game and the team almost failed the class. If the pivot into a crucial and foundational piece like the game engine had been introduced properly through mutual agreement and alongside proper learning resources it would have been a different story. In the final reflection the team wrote about how the pivot was not handled well, and how they could have made a much better game in Unity and how they had gotten tunnel visioned after the newly released tool and its features.

Hypothetical Scenario: Adaptive Leadership During AI Integration in Education

A university decides to introduce an AI-powered adaptive learning platform to improve student engagement and academic performance. The platform uses machine learning to analyze students' learning patterns, identify areas where they struggle, and adjust educational content according to their individual progress.

Initially, the university's leadership mandates that all faculty members adopt the platform within one semester. However, several challenges emerge. Some professors struggle with the new technology, students report that the AI-generated learning pathways do not always align with their learning preferences, and concerns arise about the accuracy of the system's recommendations.

Rather than enforcing the original implementation plan, the project leader adopts an adaptive learning approach. They begin collecting feedback from faculty and students, analyzing engagement data, and identifying patterns in the platform's failures. The feedback reveals that students in practical, project-based courses benefit less from the system than those studying theoretical subjects.

Based on these findings, the leader modifies the implementation strategy. Instead of requiring every department to use the same platform configuration, faculty members are allowed to customize its application according to their course requirements. Additional training sessions are introduced, and student feedback is incorporated into periodic system evaluations. The university also works with the platform developers to improve its recommendations.

Over the following semesters, faculty acceptance improves, students become more comfortable using the platform, and the university develops a more effective approach to AI integration.

The scenario demonstrates how adaptive learning in leadership involves continuously evaluating outcomes, recognizing limitations, and adjusting strategies based on real-world feedback. Rather than treating the initial implementation plan as a fixed solution, the leader uses experimentation, evidence, and collaboration to guide organizational change successfully.
